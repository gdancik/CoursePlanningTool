As you are designing an assignment, you will want to consider if the 
intention of the assignment is to be formative or summative. <b>Formative 
assignments</b> are typically those that help students build toward learning 
outcomes. These assignments are often low stakes and help the faculty to 
see where students are in terms of the skills and course materials being 
covered. <b>Summative assignments</b>, on the other hand, are intended to assess 
student performance at the end of a course unit or semester. 
Different types of assignments ask students to engage in different levels of 
cognitive work (see the Learning Outcomes section of this tool for a review
of different competencies).
<b>Standardized examinations</b>, for example, require memorization, while group projects require students
to create something collaboratively. As you develop assignments for your 
course, you will want to consider what role they play for students in terms 
of progressing towards or demonstrating achievement of learning outcomes and 
what type of cognitive process you expect students to use in completing the 
assignment. Through this process you will also want to consider the order of 
assignments and think about how you expect students to integrate their 
learning across assignments.
